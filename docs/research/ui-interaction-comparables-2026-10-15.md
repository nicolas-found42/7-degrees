# UI and interaction comparables for 7-degrees

Researched October 15, 2026. This is research for a design interview, not an approved implementation plan. Settled direction: improve production, borrow selectively from prototypes, prioritize beginners, permit structural changes, and start with browsing the whole network. The primary browsing reward is finding specific players; desktop comes first, with phone work later. Visual direction: calm and approachable with character.

## Method and limits

Sources are the products themselves and their official documentation. Basketball Reference, NBA Rotations, Dunks & Threes, Gephi Lite, and Sigma were read through Jev Browser. The flows exercised were NBA Rotations' explanation navigation, Dunks' player search, and Gephi Lite's sample loading and node search. Obsidian and Bloom findings come from official documentation, not live product testing.

“Observed” below means browser text/state or an action trace; it does not imply a complete visual, usability, accessibility, or performance audit. No frame-rate, input-latency, mobile-device, or motion measurements were taken. Documentation that promises smooth rendering is a vendor claim, not a measurement of perceived fluidity. These examples suggest design hypotheses; they do not prove that beginners will prefer a particular design.

## Proposed design hypotheses to discuss

These are recommendations derived from the comparisons, not findings that the external products establish about 7-degrees. No choice here overrides the user's remaining interview decisions.

1. **The network is the landing page.** Above it: a short purpose sentence and “Find a player.” Beside it: a small legend and optional recognizable player starting points. Give search greater prominence than discovery suggestions because locating specific players is the primary reward. The overview should already contain something legible and inviting; search helps orientation but is not a required first step.
2. **Selection explains; focus is deliberate.** A click selects a player and opens a restrained detail panel. A separate action shows teammates or moves closer. This avoids turning every inspection into an unexpected camera jump. Whether a click should also subtly recenter remains a design decision.
3. **Progressively reveal relationships.** At overview scale, emphasize meaningful structure and a limited set of labels; when a player is selected, emphasize that player's connections. Keep the full network available and label any filtered or neighborhood view clearly. Exact aggregation and layout are undecided; do not invent team/era clusters that imply relationships the data does not support.
4. **Make recovery obvious.** Keep “Whole network” or “Reset view” visible. Returning from focus should recover a recognizable map. A minimap is a candidate if navigation testing shows people losing orientation, not a mandatory extra panel.
5. **Use motion to preserve orientation.** Keep unaffected node positions stable. Animate intentional camera changes; avoid repeated refits when a drawer opens, a hover changes or search results update. Respect reduced motion. These are proposed requirements; none were measured in the comparison apps.
6. **Carry the calm visual direction through hierarchy.** Use a neutral canvas, clear player labels, limited semantic colors and one strong selected state. Reserve team colors for identity where they have a defined meaning. The research supports explicit encoding and clear grouping of controls; the exact palette, typography and decoration still need visual exploration with the user.

## What to validate in a later prototype

Ask a beginner to explain what a node and edge mean, find a familiar player, inspect a teammate relationship, return to the whole network, and find a connection between two players. Observe wrong turns and whether the canvas, search, or controls caused them. For the desktop-first pass, test mouse and keyboard paths separately, including a non-hover way to read relationship details. Check focus order, readable result lists outside the canvas and reduced motion. Record touch and narrow-screen panel work for the later phone pass; phone parity is not an initial acceptance requirement.

For fluidity, measure pan/zoom and selection on the real production graph and a representative low-power device. Watch for input lag, labels popping excessively, long layout movement and loss of camera position; do not use successful page loading or a static screenshot as evidence of smoothness. Implementation and acceptance thresholds remain for the next design round.

## Evidence review

Fetched content was screened with Jev; review flags on ordinary browser action traces and product text were inspected and contained no operative instructions directed at the research agent. Jev verified the seven consolidated factual claims covering the directly inspected basketball, Gephi and Sigma material, with no unsupported or contradicted claims. The complementary Obsidian/Bloom documentation review likewise verified its concrete feature claims; an overbroad performance/absence-of-evidence claim was flagged and excluded. This report makes no measured-fluidity claim. Application code and existing prototype changes were untouched; no code-test result is claimed for this research-only note.
